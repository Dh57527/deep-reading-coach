难度标签：四级

On many campuses, a new kind of club is quietly growing. Students bring broken lamps, torn backpacks and old radios to a small room, and volunteers help them fix the things instead of throwing them away. The clubs are usually called repair cafes, and most of them open once or twice a month.

The idea is simple. A repair cafe gives people a table, a few tools and someone who knows how to use them. Visitors watch, ask questions and often learn to do the fixing themselves. Nothing is thrown out unless it is really dead, and almost nothing costs money.

Supporters say the clubs do more than save rubbish. Many students arrive with a broken object and leave with a new skill. They also meet classmates they would never talk to in a lecture hall. For first-year students who feel lonely, a repair table can be an easy place to start a conversation.

The clubs still have problems. Volunteers are hard to keep, because fixing a radio can take a whole afternoon. Some spare parts are expensive or simply impossible to find. Even so, the number of repair cafes on campuses has doubled in the past three years, and many of them now have a waiting list.
